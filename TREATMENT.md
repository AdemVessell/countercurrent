# Treatment — a small conversation with water

One circular ivory-and-brass float rests in a square pale-stone basin. Pull it down and release: its small heave travels outward as ripples; reflected waves return and influence the float. The same live water field supports the surface, diagnostics and coupling.

Geometry: one substantial table-top basin, shallow translucent water, a finely machined float, restrained inlaid ruler marks. No coastal scenery or inherited Tideglass staging.

Light: bright soft daylight from the left, broad reflected highlights, a chalk-white gallery ground, quiet warm shadows. Water is petrol blue and clear near the rim. Brass is restrained, not mirror gold.

Camera: an elevated three-quarter view establishes the whole instrument; an authored slight approach makes the wave visible. A direct top view is available for honest inspection.

Motion: begin with a quiet depression/release, let the ripples propagate, then invite the viewer to interrupt. No endless cinematic orbit. Dragging exits the demonstration without resetting the simulation.

Surface/interface: generous editorial typography, charcoal on warm ivory, one violet accent for active input, thin rules and ample empty space. The lower edge carries immediate manipulation controls; technical evidence lives in a deliberately opened inspector.

Sound: optional procedural water-like tones if time permits; off by default and no audio asset or physical acoustic claim.

Scientific boundary: linear shallow-water waves plus an energy-derived reciprocal vertical coupling to one heave degree of freedom. Horizontal placement and the user's hand are external forcing. No resolved hull, splash or breaking-wave claim. Rendered water optics are an approximation and do not constitute optical validation.
